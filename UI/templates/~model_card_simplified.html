Name: The technology called GordonAI.

Overview: The GordonAI artificial intelligence (AI) package uses the mdeberta-v3-base model to analyze sentiments, detect emotions, and classify facts in different languages. This model is specifically trained on a dataset for fact-checking to categorize text into Disinformation, Hoax, FakeNews, or TrueNews groups. The mdeberta-v3-base model, which builds upon BERT and RoBERTa technologies, includes disentangled attention and an improved mask decoder, trained on a large 80GB dataset. DeBERTa V3 enhances efficiency by utilizing ELECTRA-Style pre-training with Gradient Disentangled Embedding Sharing, which boosts performance on tasks related to understanding natural language. DeBERTa's structure uses disentangled attention to calculate attention weights based on content and positions in text, outperforming human performance on SuperGLUE benchmarks. The Enhanced Mask Decoder in DeBERTa incorporates absolute word positions for masked language prediction, thus improving accuracy by considering both content and position information. By employing a virtual adversarial training approach, the model enhances generalization, resulting in better performance on a variety of natural language processing (NLP) tasks. GordonAI, powered by the DeBERTa model, is highly effective in fact-checking, sentiment analysis, and NLP activities, with potential uses in combatting misinformation, improving language comprehension, and boosting accuracy in text classification. The Enhanced Mask Decoder (EMD) in DeBERTa factors in absolute word positions, enhancing prediction accuracy by considering both relative and absolute positions. The Scale-Invariant Fine-Tuning (SiFT) technique enhances training stability by adjusting normalized word embeddings, particularly useful for larger DeBERTa models during fine-tuning for downstream NLP tasks.

Description: The dataset consists of 29,320 entries organized into five columns: 'dataset', 'doc', 'claim', 'label', and 'contamination_identifier'. The 'dataset' column specifies where the data comes from, with 'RAGTruth' being the most common source at 16,371 entries, followed by 'ExpertQA' with 3,702 entries. In the 'doc' column, you can find text samples covering topics like child prodigies, controversial figures, and events such as pool draining and memorial commemorations. The 'claim' column contains statements that either support or contradict the information in the 'doc' column, discussing topics like sports, activism, and family outings. The 'label' column indicates if a claim is true (labeled as 1) or false (labeled as 0). There is an imbalance in the labels, with 22,832 instances labeled as true and 6,488 instances labeled as false. The 'contamination_identifier' column seems to contain unique identifiers for each entry, likely used for tracking or ensuring data accuracy. This dataset focuses on verifying claims against provided information from various sources. The label imbalance could make it challenging for machine learning models, as they might tend to predict claims as true due to the larger true class. Further analysis and adjustments to the model may be needed to handle this imbalance and improve the accuracy of claim verification.
